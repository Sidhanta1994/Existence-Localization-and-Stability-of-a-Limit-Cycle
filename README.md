# Existence, Localization, and Stability of a Limit Cycle in a Class of Benchmark Biomolecular Oscillators

Oscillatory behaviour is important in multiple biological contexts. However, the inherent nonlinearity and high dimensionality of mathematical models in biology makes proving the existence and the localization of limit cycle oscillations challenging. Here, we provided an elementary proof for the existence and a method for rigorously localizing the oscillatory solutions in a class of benchmark biomolecular oscillators. To construct the proof, we used a geometric approach based on Brouwer’s Fixed Point theorem. We constructed a toroidal-like manifold within a positively invariant set by removing the hypervolume containing the fixed point and the trajectories converging to it along its stable manifold. We showed that the vector field describing the system dynamics maps a cross section of the toroidal-like manifold onto itself. The existence of a limit cycle solution in this manifold was guaranteed by Brouwer’s Fixed Point theorem. For different sets of initial conditions in these cross-sections, we used an interval-based Reachability Analysis to localize the oscillatory solution. The stability of the limit cycle was established through an interval-based Poincar\'e Map analysis. The Jacobian of the discrete Poincar\'e Map was calculated over the localized cross-section using the augmented state-variational system. The sub-elements of the previously localized region were identified where the eigenvalues of the Jacobian of the Poincar\'e Map lay within the unit circle, guaranteeing contraction of the return map and hence stability. This work establishes a comprehensive workflow for analyzing oscillatory dynamics in nonlinear biomolecular systems. The methodology provides an elegant framework for demonstrating the existence of limit cycles, rigorously localizing the region of existence, and verifying their stability through an interval-based Poincar\'e Map analysis.
